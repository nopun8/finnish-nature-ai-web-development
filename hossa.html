Hossa National Park

Hossa National Park in northern Suomussalmi is known for clear waters, esker and lake landscapes, extensive trails and a wide range of outdoor activities.

Nature and Landscape

Hossa protects a landscape of ridges, hills and small waters shaped by a long relationship between people and nature. The area combines geology, forests, lakes and cultural layers.

Hiking and Routes

  • The park has about 90 km of trails.
  • Many routes are relatively easy to walk.
  • Circular and connecting trails can be combined for longer hikes.
  • Trails are not maintained for hiking during winter.
  • Use official route descriptions for distance and accessibility.

Activities

  • Hiking
  • Mountain biking on permitted routes
  • Fishing where permitted
  • Paddling
  • Skiing and winter activities

Facilities and Services

  • Visitor-centre services
  • Huts and open wilderness huts
  • Tent and lean-to shelter options
  • Campervan/caravan and nearby accommodation
  • Accessible services on selected routes

How to Get There

Hossa is about 240 km from Oulu by road. Summer and autumn coach connections can serve Hossa; winter bus access may require nearby stops. The nearest railway station identified by Luontoon is Kajaani, about 170 km away. Verify current schedules.

Safety

  • Trails are not maintained for winter hiking.
  • Water activities require suitable skills and flotation equipment.
  • Remote routes need food, water and navigation planning.
  • Follow local fire rules.
  • Protect archaeological and cultural sites.

Recommended Equipment

  • Hiking footwear
  • Weatherproof layers
  • Map and compass
  • Charged phone and power bank
  • First-aid kit
  • Headlamp
  • Food and water
  • Personal flotation device for paddling

Seasonal Planning

  • Summer: broad access to hiking, cycling and paddling.
  • Autumn: cooler and seasonal transport may operate.
  • Winter: trails are not maintained for hiking.
  • Spring: thaw and changing water conditions require care.

Rules and Responsible Hiking

  • Do not litter or damage archaeological sites.
  • Do not damage plants, soil or bedrock.
  • Keep pets controlled.
  • Respect wildlife and other visitors.
  • Follow activity-specific rules for fishing, cycling, fires and drones.
